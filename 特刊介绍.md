Artificial Intelligence, Data Investment and the Digital Economy
Submission deadline: 28 February 2027
The Economic Modelling journal invites submissions for a forthcoming SpecialIssue on the economic implications of Artificial Intelligence in the Data
Economy.

Special issue information:

The rapid development and diffusion of Artificial Intelligence (AI), increasingfirm investment in data assets, and data-driven production models are reshaping economies worldwide. AI systems, data investment, platform economies, cloud computing, big dataanalytics, and digital infrastructures are transforming firm organization, industrial structures and firm networks, labor markets, R&D and innovation processes, the banking system, financial markets, and macroeconomic dynamics. The emergence of generative AI represents a significant shift that could redefine productivity and creativity; however, its successful integration
into the economy relies on the continuous generation and strategic use of high quality data. At the same time, the rise of the data economy raises fundamental questions regarding productivity growth, labor market outcomes, mark-up dynamics, market concentration, income inequality, geographical disparities,
structural change, and economic resilience. Differences in digital capabilities, human capital, institutional quality, and regulatory frameworks may generate heterogeneous effects across countries and regions, influencing patterns of convergence and divergence in the global economy. The geopolitics of digitalization — including technological fragmentation, strategic dependencies, and the international contestation over
digital standards and infrastructure — adds a further layer of complexity to these dynamics.

This Special Issue aims to advance theoretical and empirical research on the economic consequences of AI, digitalization, and the data economy at both the micro and macro levels. We welcome contributions addressing, but not limited to, the following topics:

AI adoption, development, and productivity dynamics
Data as an asset and related measurement challenges
Drivers and effects of data investment and AI
Generative AI, data investment, and the labor market
AI and the data value chain
R&D and returns to innovation: the role of AI, software, and data
Digital transformation, markups, and market structure
Data, intangible capital, knowledge spillovers, and growth
AI and automation, skills, employment, and factor income wage inequality
AI, data, and global value chains
AI, data, and financial stability
AI, data, and economic resilience
AI, technological specialization, and structural change
Competition policy and data regulation in digital markets
AI, digitalization, and the environmental dimension of the green transition
Both theoretical and empirical contributions are welcome. Empirical studies should rely on sound methodological approaches, with clearly articulated identification strategies, where relevant, and appropriate robustness analyses. Contributions that employ rigorous quantitative frameworks — particularly those based on causal inference or structural methods — are especially encouraged.
